# Product contract

## Target product and first buyer

Target approved 16 October 2026: **an owned Slack replacement for a US small or mid-size team**. Nook owns workplace messaging: channels, DMs and threads, dependable apps and push on every device, voice/video/screen sharing, an app and bot platform, shared external channels, and decisions and commitments that accountable agents follow through. A team using **Slack, Microsoft Teams or Google Chat** must be able to move its daily communication job here. **Campfire (37signals ONCE), Zulip and Mattermost** set an additional switching bar for owned operation, low-friction conversation and topic continuity. Their migrations are explicit obligations, not claims that each source exports every object.

The contract describes the product being built toward. `done`, `partial` and `missing` at the end describe current code/test coverage, not a reduced product or a shipped-service claim. US English, USD operating costs, US time zones/daylight saving and US business identity/privacy/records norms are the first-buyer defaults. Native clients, provider agreements, media transport, compliance custody and app approvals are engineering obligations, never exclusions.

Nook remains its own product. [Envelope](https://github.com/just-every/run-envelope) is the RunEditRun product for small-business payroll; [Pegboard](https://github.com/just-every/run-pegboard) for work and project management; [Porthole](https://github.com/just-every/run-porthole) for observability (metrics, logs, traces and alerts). Nook’s audit log is access-control history, not observability. [Vector](https://github.com/just-every/run-vector) owns project work, [HappyToHelp](https://github.com/just-every/run-happytohelp) support, [Uptime](https://github.com/just-every/run-uptime) and [Lamp](https://github.com/just-every/run-lamp) their alerts/operational signals, and [Chest](https://github.com/just-every/run-chest) file ownership. Nook connects their records to conversations; no sibling, market gap or office-suite product is absorbed. Sharing implementation is welcome; merging the products is not.

## Published core clauses

Each clause is `- **ID — Title.**` followed by its text. NOOK-001 to NOOK-015 are the clauses earlier documents call C1 to C15 (NOOK-014 is C14, for example); a reference to Cn means NOOK-0nn.

- **NOOK-001 — Current identity and conversation membership (C1).** Every private API validates current active identity and current conversation membership. Search/replay/outcomes use the same policy. Workspace admins cannot read arbitrary DMs.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Through signed-in HTTP, SSR, search, replay and generated loaders, revoke or expire a session and remove a participant; every subsequent private read/write is refused, including a workspace admin attempting another person’s DM.

- **NOOK-002 — Atomic messages, retries and replay (C2).** Messages commit atomically with per-conversation event sequence and client intent key. Identical retries return the same message; changed retry payload conflicts. Replay pages preserve ordering; edits use compare-and-swap versions; deletes tombstone and erase original body from live revisions.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Send through the conversation API, repeat the same intent after a lost acknowledgment, then change its payload; observe one ordered message, the original retry result and a conflict for changed intent. Race edits and delete; replay shows current tombstones without deleted bodies.

- **NOOK-003 — Fixed DMs and channel membership (C3).** DMs have fixed participants. Channel owners control membership. Removal revokes reads and writes immediately, including replay and search. New members currently see entire channel history; UI and docs must disclose this.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Create channel/private/DM conversations through the app, add a channel member after earlier messages, and observe full-history disclosure and access. Remove that member and deny history/search/replay/files immediately; refuse DM participant changes and unrelated-admin DM access.

- **NOOK-004 — Decisions and commitments (C4).** A decision has a human author, conversation scope and immutable version history. Only its author revises it with version comparison. A commitment can become accepted only through the assignee's action. Completion is reported, never labeled verified.
  **Acceptance evidence:** From a discussion, record a decision and propose a commitment. Another participant cannot revise the decision or accept for the assignee; the assignee accepts the exact version and reports completion. Reopen history and see human authorship, source context and reported status.

- **NOOK-005 — Read cursors and attention (C5).** Read cursors persist per user and conversation, are monotonic and cannot exceed durable frontier. Attention/unread is separate from accepted responsibility.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Read a thread from two sessions, reconnect one, and observe monotonic unread recovery with own sends excluded. Refuse read markers beyond the durable frontier; closing Attention does not accept work or mark unseen messages read.

- **NOOK-006 — Offline restore (C6).** Restore is offline into an empty database with version and checksum verification and SQLite integrity checking. Current backups contain private content, never live sessions; password hashes belong only to explicitly supported legacy recovery inputs and never become new sign-in authority. No external effects on restore.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Use rer export/reset/import into an empty stopped installation, complete explicit identity recovery, and compare conversations, memberships, outcome versions, attention and downloaded file bytes. A populated destination, altered checksum or invalid graph is refused; no old delivery or session revives.

- **NOOK-007 — Safe rendering and local binding (C7).** Human text renders inertly. No message, plugin, link or AI output can trigger arbitrary code, client-side or credentialed fetching, or provider effects; the only server-side link fetch is NOOK-068's bounded, SSRF-safe preview service. Authorized app/agent/meeting effects require the explicit scoped doors in NOOK-022–030 and NOOK-054–059, not ambient conversation authority. Bounded bodies, session expiry, origin validation for mutations and loopback-only development binding remain required; production requires HTTPS. New sign-in uses invited Base magic links; legacy password hashes remain offline recovery inputs only.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Post active markup and malformed/oversized bodies through public APIs; markup stays inert and invalid input fails without writes. Cross-origin mutations, foreign sessions and unsafe local origins fail; production output excludes dev admission.

- **NOOK-008 — Evidence limits (C8).** The complete target in this contract remains an obligation until its acceptance evidence exists. Local tests, synthetic imports and substituted network edges establish only their bounded paths; hosted scale, legal/compliance operation, real inbox/device receipt, native push and live provider integrations require separate evidence. Missing proof changes the status, never the scope. Delivery/provider acceptance, device display, human reading and action are distinct facts.
  **Acceptance evidence:** For a release candidate, reproduce the documented local public-door journeys, then separately record hosted multi-device, provider, migration and capacity results before those target clauses become done. Failed or unmeasured end states remain visible.

- **NOOK-009 — Backup and export (C9).** Two full backup paths ship, neither an ordinary app-admin export. On a local install, the filesystem operator uses local-only `rer export` and `rer import` on a stopped checkout; operator disk access explicitly bypasses app privacy. For a hosted install, the [hosted backup/export path](docs/backups.md#export-a-hosted-nook) uses the owner’s `/export` download or the documented `node --input-type=module` command calling `scripts/local/remote-export.mjs` from a checkout with dependencies installed; it exports the authoritative D1 database records and actual private R2 object bytes into an owner-held verified archive with its erasure journal. Nook's `/export` and `/api/portability` doors admit only an active owner's current session and authorize no import. [Base's hosted-data rule](https://github.com/runeditrun/base/blob/main/export/RUN-LOCALLY.md#if-hosted-deployment-stops) distinguishes this from local-only `rer export`, which refuses a hosted origin: a platform D1 SQL export alone omits R2 files and cannot satisfy this full-backup guarantee. Restore is always local, into an empty destination, and validates table identities, foreign references, schema ledger and event/read frontiers. Archives exclude sessions and sign-in tokens. User-readable and policy-scoped exits are required by NOOK-047; this clause retains the full-backup guarantee and its explicit operator-custody exception.
  **Acceptance evidence:** **Local:** Stop the install, export with `rer export`, import into an empty stopped local install, complete explicit identity recovery and re-export; compare equivalent records, private media bytes and erasure journal. **Hosted:** Sign in as an active owner, follow the hosted backup/export path through `/export` and the documented helper command to download D1 records plus R2 bytes into an owner-held archive, import it into an empty stopped local install and complete identity recovery; compare records, file bytes, rebuilt search and erasure journal. Reject non-owner/expired hosted sessions and any remote restore. Inspect both archives for private records and media, with live credentials, devices and pending deliveries excluded.

- **NOOK-010 — Commitment revision and cancellation (C10).** Material text/deadline revision requires renewed assignee acceptance at the exact new version. Assignee and conversation are fixed; changing assignee means explicit cancellation and a fresh proposal, and scope cannot move across conversations. Cancellation is explicit, terminal and versioned; authors may cancel proposals/accepted work but cannot claim assignee completion. Deleted source refs become null; event rows preserve opaque sequence/object facts, never message bodies. Replay clients fetch current object state under current ACL and interpret tombstones.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Accept a proposal and revise its text/deadline: renewed acceptance is required at the new version. Cancel and create a new proposal to change the fixed assignee. Refuse stale acceptance, cross-conversation moves and author impersonation of completion; cancellation stays terminal in history and deleted source refs reveal no body.

- **NOOK-011 — Cached retries, synchronization and complete history (C11).** Cached retry responses require current ACL before lookup. Reads synchronize monotonically across sessions; replay cursors indicate per-conversation sequence and bounded catch-up, not read receipts. History pages must cover the complete tested log rather than silently truncate.
  **Acceptance evidence:** Page beyond the tested history window and reconnect after missed edit/delete events. Repeat a previously successful intent after membership revocation; it must be denied before cached response lookup, with no missing or duplicated durable history.

- **NOOK-012 — Private attachments (C12).** Attachments are 1 byte–25 MiB per file, five ordered files per message, with at most 50 pending/staged native uploads per uploader and no aggregate workspace byte quota. Actual bytes reside in private R2; D1 holds metadata and opaque object keys. Plain UTF-8 text, signature-checked PNG/JPEG/PDF only; forced attachment downloads plus private inline PNG/JPEG previews after structural byte validation and opt-in plain-text previews bounded to 64 KiB; no inline PDF preview or virus-scanning claim. Staged uploads last24h for binding/read eligibility, are uploader-only under current conversation ACL, and do not create attention. Unused staged bytes remain until explicitly removed. Binding uploader-owned same-conversation IDs and message/intent/event is atomic; exact ID order belongs to retry identity. Downloads recheck current membership; delete erases live bytes/name/checksum, and message deletion cascades. The media baseline archivev2 carries actual bytes plus hashes, state and binding; historical archivev3 retains these guarantees plus C13 triage and supports explicit prior-v1/v2 migration; current normalized Base portability archives retain the same custody guarantees, as described below. Backups/WAL/remnants retain their stated physical-erasure limits. These limits are the shipped baseline, not the target: NOOK-061 widens admission to any file type up to 1 GiB with in-app previews, malware scanning and admin file-type policy while keeping every custody guarantee in this clause.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Upload and bind ordered private files through the app, preview genuine PNG/JPEG and download exact bytes as an authorized participant. Deny outsiders and removed members, reject active/corrupt/oversized input, recover lost-put acknowledgments and verify deletion and backup round-trip.

- **NOOK-013 — Calm attention (C13).** Calm attention is explicit per-user triage, independent of unread and outcome authority. GET/PATCH `/api/conversations/:id/preferences` accepts boolean `muted`/`followed`, requires current membership, and returns those choices plus followed root-thread IDs. POST `/api/messages/:id/follow {follow:boolean}` targets a live root thread under current ACL. Choices persist across sessions; changes affect future routing only. Muting suppresses routine follows, never explicit mentions or commitment proposals. Automatic following when a member authors, replies in or is mentioned in a thread, and of every thread in a one-to-one DM, is NOOK-072 and writes this same followed state; an unfollow through this door also ends an automatic follow.

  Message sends accept ordered unique `mentions` (at most50 current member IDs), boolean `urgent`, and `urgentReason` (1–240 characters when urgent). Urgent requests require explicit recipients. All current channel members can see the message's Notified names, Urgent marker and urgent reason, including members who were not notified. Defaults `[]`, `false`, `null` join immutable intent identity. Identical committed retries survive recipient removal without rerouting; new sends revalidate recipients. Attention creation is atomic with durable message/outcome events and stores opaque references, not copied bodies. Precedence: urgent-request, mention, thread-follow, conversation-follow. Proposals and material revisions route to assignees; assignee actions route updates to authors. Self-authored events do not create self-attention.

  GET `/api/attention` returns current-ACL hydrated active items, `deferredCount`, `dndUntil`. Deleted sources hydrate tombstones, with no urgent-reason copy. GET/PATCH `/api/settings {dndUntil:epochMilliseconds|null}` controls quiet time. Quiet time defers routine items while urgent in-app requests remain visible; external delivery follows C14 email and C15 Web Push, and there is no paging guarantee. POST `/api/attention/:id {action:done|snooze,until?:epochMilliseconds}` requires item ownership and current ACL. Quiet/snooze deadlines must be future times within seven days. Snoozed items become eligible when due; routine items remain deferred during quiet time. Done changes only triage. Reading or accepting a commitment does not automatically close attention, and closing attention never accepts work.

  Schema5 preserves prior ledgers and appends explicit defaults to live prior intent payloads; erased markers remain erased. Archivev3 retains follows/preferences/quiet time/triage and validates source/event/scope references. Explicit v1/v2 imports add empty attention tables and normalize prior retry identity. Old-format archives cannot carry nonempty new attention state. Root SQLite v3 and native D1 recovery formats require an explicit separately verified mapping; a source manifest alone does not establish compatibility.

  C13 admission clarification: defaults apply only to omitted mentions/urgent fields, not explicit null. Explicit mentions:null or urgent:null is400 with no committed event/message/attention. Recovered dnd_until must be null or a safe integer; expired valid integers remain legal recovery values. Quiet-time domain checks precede database insertion.

  **Acceptance evidence:** At 1440 and 390, follow a thread, mute its channel, send explicit mentions/urgent requests, snooze and complete triage, and enter quiet time. Restart and sign in elsewhere: preferences persist, due items return under current ACL, and neither triage nor reads accepts responsibility. Automatic follows from authoring, replying, mentions and one-to-one DMs are proved under NOOK-072; an unfollow through `POST /api/messages/:id/follow {follow:false}` ends one.

- **NOOK-014 — Email notifications (C14).** Email notifications reach a member away from the browser under this delivery contract. Emailable events are an explicit mention, an urgent request, a commitment proposal or material revision to its assignee, and a completion report to its author; routine thread or conversation follows, reactions, replies without a mention, accept/cancel updates and imported history are never emailed. The destination is the member's own sign-in email (an active account, verified by its sign-in link); there is no free-form address. Every member's email is off until that member turns it on in their own settings, per account: one choice covers mentions and commitments, a second covers urgent requests, with a separate choice to let urgent requests through quiet time. GET/PATCH `/api/notify {mentions,urgent,urgentQuiet:boolean}` reads and changes the caller's choices. Turning a choice off stops the next email: each queued item is checked again when its email is built. Every email carries a link that turns email off without a session (`/notify/off`: opening it shows one button and changes nothing, because mail scanners open links; the button turns all three choices off) and a link to the signed-in settings.

  Queue rows are written in the same batch as the attention rows, only for recipients who turned the matching email on, and hold opaque references (recipient, conversation, actor, message or commitment, reason), never message text, file names or addresses. The ChatHub alarm drains them; pending rows keep it armed, so delivery needs no open socket and a restarted Worker resumes from D1. When an email is built, nothing is sent to an inactive member, about a conversation the member can no longer read, about a deleted or erased source, after the choice was turned off, or for an item the member already marked done or a mention they already read; each is canceled with that reason. Quiet time (C13 `dndUntil`) holds every item until it ends; an urgent request goes through quiet time only when its recipient chose that. Each event is queued once per recipient (keyed by its attention item), so identical retries add nothing; a burst within five seconds folds into one email, and a recipient gets at most one email per conversation per ten minutes, with later items folded into a count. A failed send is retried after 15 s, 1 min, 5 min, 30 min and 2 h, then given up; a definite refusal is not retried, and a send the provider did not confirm, or that a stopped Worker may have made, is never repeated. A send being retried or given up shows as "email not delivered" with its reason class (not set up, refused, rate limited, not confirmed) to its recipient, to the member whose message caused it and to the owner for seven days, never as a silent success. Emails name the sender, the conversation (`#name`, or "a direct conversation") and a link; they never carry message text, file names, urgent reasons or commitment wording. This no-text form is today's behavior and the `sender` level an owner policy can force; a member's opt-in to message text in email is NOOK-062's obligation.

  Hosted mail goes through the same configured sender as sign-in (the owner deployment's provider). Local development sends and stores no email and exposes no mail viewer. Tests fake only the provider network edge. Accepted by the mail provider is not proof of inbox receipt, and a test provider's acceptance is not live delivery. Urgent stays an in-app request, not paging; Web Push follows C15; there is no SMS or native push. Archives carry members' choices but not the queue, so a restore sends nothing old; erasure removes a member's choices and queued items and an erased message's items.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Run the emitted-Worker notification journey through sign-in and settings with only the provider network edge substituted: opt in, mention/propose, fold bursts, hold quiet items, cancel read/deleted/revoked items, restart a retry, expose refusal/uncertainty and use the scanner-safe unsubscribe POST. Inspect that mail contains no private body or file name.

## Native consolidation

The earlier root native Worker used schema4 and archivev4; current app authority is normalized D1 and private R2 as described below. Applied D1 migrations0001–0003 are unchanged;0004 adds immutable external history with no historical events, unread, attention or work acceptance. Historical root ledger1–6/archivev1–v4 and native archivev2–v3 are explicit recovery inputs; imported receipts/provenance/media remain independently recoverable. The retired Node/SQLite runtime and composition outputs are no longer application entry points. C1–C13 remain obligations, including the historical transitions described above. Historical document archives retain their original admission bounds. Normalized authority removes the whole-document size ceiling and never prunes history silently.

## Sign-in and identity

C1, C3, C9 and C11 remain current-identity/current-membership obligations. Every
private API, SSR document and workspace loader resolves a verified Base operator-auth
session and stable product ID. Owner/admin roles do not bypass conversation ACLs.
OA session expiry/revocation/operator-version fences are repeated in D1 mutation
CAS and guarded reads because auth-table changes do not advance workspace
revision. Reads do not initialize or modify the product document.

C6/C7's legacy password wording describes old recovery inputs only. New accounts
use explicit owner bootstrap and invited magic links; open registration/password
login are removed. Confirmation GET/HEAD never consumes a link; native POST
consumes once. Native and cross-device sign-in (finding the installation, managed deployment, email codes, universal and app links, sign in on mobile from desktop, personal providers and system-browser SSO) is NOOK-074. Local development uses per-checkout-key-gated dev sessions:
owner entry through `RER_OPEN` and `/setup` precedes fixed local persona entry in
the admitted browser. Personas do not accept arbitrary email invitations.
Local development sends and stores no mail and exposes no mail viewer.
Configured production auth requires HTTPS, a supported mail provider and trusted
platform client IP; it retains default Base rate limits. Default Base roles are
owner/admin/member.

Normal archives use Base portability with semantic product validation, integrity
checking, empty-target restore and explicit credential exclusions. Legacy archive
v1-v4 conversion remains a separate recovery boundary. Prior password users have
no inferred mailbox: an explicit complete stable-ID/mailbox/role mapping and
active owner selection atomically establish auth authority in a new offline copy.
Multiple owner roles are preserved; recovery selects one explicitly active owner.
Inactive IDs, all private references and original recovery bytes are preserved.
No fresh owner automatically receives prior conversations.

## Native normalized storage (Part 3)

C1–C11 and C13 remain obligations. App reads and mutations use indexed row-level D1 storage after an explicit offline authority cutover. Every mutation batch fences the current Base operator ID, version, active status, unrevoked unexpired session and current conversation membership inside SQL. Events allocate one contiguous sequence in the same batch as the message/revision/outcome/attention/attachment changes. Internal event actors are retained where known; public replay remains opaque. Membership confers historical access, including fixed DM membership; a workspace admin receives no extra DM access.

C12 admits 1 byte–25 MiB per file, up to five ordered attachments per message, and at most 50 pending/staged native uploads per uploader. There is no product-wide aggregate media-byte quota; actual D1/R2/platform/account limits still apply. Bytes live only in the private R2 binding, never a base64 D1 cell. Binary requests are bounded by consumed bytes; legacy JSON file requests remain explicitly bounded. Filename, declared MIME, signatures and UTF-8 checks remain required, and these checks are not virus scanning (NOOK-061 adds scanning). Native upload intent reserves an opaque object key durably before put, then finalizes with fresh authority. Retries after lost acknowledgments use the reservation; only finalized staged uploads bind. Staging expires after 24 hours for binding and download, and unused files require explicit removal. Deletes tombstone metadata and persist the physical-delete obligation before R2 erase. Recovery retries the erase; it never compensates an uncertain finalize by deleting a potentially bound object. Download responses force attachment disposition with no-store/nosniff. A separate PNG/JPEG preview endpoint validates the image container, rechecks current membership after reading private bytes and returns inline image bytes with private/no-store, nosniff and a sandbox CSP. The client renders a revocable blob URL in an img element. Plain-text files also have an opt-in private preview bounded to 64 KiB, rendered as inert text with a visible shortened-preview label when truncated; exact original bytes remain downloadable. PDF files remain downloads; there are no public object URLs.

History and replay seek indexed `(conversation,sequence)` frontiers. Author-aware unread counts native messages after the persisted read frontier, excluding the reader's own messages and all non-message events. Sending does not advance the read frontier.

Web Drafts & sent lists this browser's account- and installation-isolated drafts in currently accessible conversations, including thread context and unconfirmed delivery state. Its Scheduled section reuses schedule editing and cancellation; Sent pages the caller's messages from the last 30 days, newest first, with current ACL and current revisions. Draft enumeration pages current conversation access; the workspace conversation list also enumerates all currently authorized conversations without a 1,000-entry cap. Web composition provides Markdown source with accessible rich preview, typed-format conversion and undo, person/channel mentions, emoji, text export, exact-byte previewable snippets and resumable split sends. Built-in slash commands use current-authority APIs and private confirmations. Profiles, chosen time zones, expiring statuses, optional typing/presence, field privacy, a fully paged 5,001-person directory, private sidebar sections, explicit mark unread and the device-kept "Your workspaces" menu with independent sign-in per installation ship. NOOK-017 still requires native-client draft/status/workspace-switch journeys, calling/GIF service effects for `/huddle` and `/gif`, app-command ordering/name reservation and Slack-compatible length errors under NOOK-021/022/025/063/070; no native workspace picker is claimed.

Search combines Unicode terms of every length, exact phrases, token prefixes (`*`), case-insensitive substrings and exclusions (`-`). Typed modifiers appear as removable chips and cover channel/person, participant, conversation type, time-zone date ranges, links, reactions, threads, saved items, pins and files. A positive non-prefix ASCII term of at least three characters uses one materialized FTS5 trigram candidate set per request; other queries scan eligible history under the same filters and current ACL. Current native and published imported file names and extracted plain-text/text-bearing PDF contents are searched from private R2 bytes, with live source and membership checks before results return. Unsupported or unindexed contents are named explicitly; extraction is bounded to 25 MiB of input, 100 PDF pages and 1 MiB of text. The emitted Worker journey pages an authenticated 80,000-message corpus completely without duplicates or omissions. Search pages examine at most 2,000 eligible candidates and return at most 100 authorized results with an opaque continuation; a sparse page is not a declaration that no later matches exist. Native clients and wider file admission remain NOOK-021 and NOOK-061 obligations.

Workspace and conversation Files views page native and published imported files newest first, filter by type/person and open authorized download, image preview and source context; their directory-based filter pickers share the current 1,000-entry directory limit. File-content search is in Search, rather than these Files views. Attention is paged at 100 items with an explicit continuation and page-scoped deferred count. The workspace actor snapshot and the Files filter pickers still cap at 1,000; the workspace conversation list has no such cap, and the separately paged people directory and Drafts & sent access enumeration are not subject to the actor-snapshot cap. The remaining directory limits do not imply an unlimited workspace claim. NOOK-049 requires every directory and the channel browser to page and search completely at its stated capacity.

`/api/live` carries only authorized conversation/frontier hints (and an empty-workspace refresh control), never event bodies, actor IDs or recipients. D1 replay is authoritative. Durable Object frontier comparison repairs a lost final post-commit hint, and reconnect always catches up through replay. Current session and ACL checks close revoked subscribers. The live channel delivers nothing outside the browser; C14 email is drained by the same Durable Object's alarm.

Schema migration 0010 appends normalized tables without changing 0001–0003. Existing documents stay explicitly authoritative and application traffic is paused until the offline converter validates all references, counts and R2 bytes, atomically marks normalized authority and removes the legacy document. Historical archive conversion is explicit; native legacy archive formats remain recovery inputs. Normalized backups use actual Base portability over D1 plus R2, retain stable product actors, exclude credentials/sessions/live effects, reject pending object work, and rebuild derived search after restore. Restore requires explicit identity mapping before bootstrap.

- **DOCS-001 — Public documentation from Markdown.** Public user documentation is rendered from Markdown sources declared in `docs/site.json`. Every root and `docs/` Markdown file is published or has a reason for remaining GitHub-only; missing files, links and heading anchors fail the build. Raw HTML in a page is reported and never rendered.

- **ERASURE-001 — Member, message and imported-author erasure.** Current owners/admins may erase accessible channel messages; roles never open an operator DM door. Authors retain their own delete/remove actions. Owners may erase eligible other members; admins only accounts with the member role, including guest access; self and the last active owner are refused. Every destructive commit guards current session, role and scope. Count-only preview, explicit confirmation and a subject-free receipt accompany operator erasure. Authored content, credentials and selected import copies are erased; authored work retains only scope/kind/version count/state. Other authors retain work and state without erased assignee/action attribution. Web Push routing records are removed with the member they belong to and with the message, outcome or member that caused them (NOOK-015, NOOK-073); none is a structural tombstone. Separate credential-free structural tombstones preserve threads, counters, fixed DM audiences and immutable import ownership; audit attribution and subject references are independently anonymized. D1 atomically records public Base erasure fences and durable blob obligations before R2 deletion. Completion requires verified blob absence, including resolution of uncertain puts; larger work remains pending with bounded continuation. Latest archives carry the journal and restore normally. Old archives are refused by nonempty-destination guards or the latest journal; a virgin destination without a later journal cannot recognize them. External archives, offline copies, provider backups/Time Travel and physical remnants remain outside logical erasure.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Through Team and message erasure doors, preview counts, confirm and resume a pending job. Try an unrelated DM, an insufficient role, self and last owner; deny each. Remove native/imported content and verified R2 bytes, preserve only declared structural tombstones, anonymize audit, and refuse an older archive when the latest erasure journal is supplied.

- **NOOK-015 — Web Push (C15).** Web Push is a separate opt-in channel under C14's current-identity, current-ACL, source, read/done, quiet-time, retry, visible-failure and no-paging rules. Migration 0023 preserves existing email rows and choices. **Defaults for a new member:** once the member grants browser notification permission, and without opening settings, DMs, mentions (including NOOK-065 broadcast mentions), keyword hits (NOOK-032), call invites (NOOK-022) and replies in followed threads (including NOOK-072 automatic follows) are on, matching Slack's default of desktop and mobile notifications for DMs, mentions and keywords; urgent-through-quiet starts off. Each member may change any of them, and owners set the organization defaults under NOOK-060. GET/PATCH `/api/notify` adds `push:{mentions,urgent,urgentQuiet,direct,threads,keywords,calls:boolean}`. Mentions covers the same commitment events as email. Ordinary DMs and followed-thread replies can push without changing email or in-app Attention. Conversation-follow for Attention alone never pushes; only a conversation set to the NOOK-032 `all new messages` level pushes each new root message and channel-broadcast thread reply. Explicit urgent and mention reasons take precedence and require their own choice; a routine followed reply precedes ordinary DM when both are enabled. Routine follows and DMs respect mute; self-authored and imported events never push.

  POST `/api/devices {transport:"webpush",subscription:{endpoint,keys:{p256dh,auth},expirationTime?:number|null}}` registers only the caller's browser subscription under the current session. Each delivery rechecks the device's original session, active version, membership, preferences, native source and unread/triage state. Expired or revoked sessions and provider-expired subscriptions retire the device.

  One durable queue row per event, recipient and device prevents duplicate alerts from overlapping reasons and successful-device repeats during another device's retry. Email and push have independent five-second gathering and ten-minute conversation folding windows; push windows are per device. The first due eligible item gathers the already-queued eligible burst, including items still in their initial gathering period; future retries, snoozes and quiet-held items stay deferred. A counted push uses one opaque delivery ID for the whole burst, and remains current while any folded item remains current. A conditional sending lease becomes visibly not-confirmed if interrupted, and uncertain network sends are never repeated. Failure classes show to the recipient, the sender and the owner for seven days; terminal delivery queue rows last thirty days, while NOOK-073's held, released and canceled routing records are retained as that clause states. Read and done transactions cancel pending push. Snoozes and quiet time defer delivery; only the separately enabled urgent-through-quiet choice overrides quiet time.

  POST `/api/devices/presence {id,page,visible:boolean}` records a current-session visible-page heartbeat. Visible presence alone holds no Web Push; NOOK-073's cross-device active-elsewhere rule governs Web Push exactly as native push: a focused page with recent input is an active desktop client, Web Push to the member's phones and tablets is held while they are active on desktop and released or canceled under NOOK-073, and the active client itself shows the alert in-app. Open sockets alone never count as presence. Foreground pages renew every fifteen seconds, hidden pages release, and the alarm automatically releases expired forty-five-second presence. No browser page is needed for delivery. GET `/api/devices/sync` supplies current opaque delivered-notification IDs and badge count for foreground synchronization; the count includes unread active attention and ordinary push-enabled DM events, deduplicated across devices.

  Web Push uses RFC 8291 encrypted payloads and RFC 8292 VAPID. The instance generates its key pair once on first authenticated use, storing the private key AES-GCM encrypted under a key derived from SESSION_SECRET, and sends only its public key to browsers. The encrypted payload contains sender/conversation display wording (DMs use "a direct conversation"), an authenticated same-origin app link with opaque conversation/message IDs, opaque recipient/delivery IDs, notification tag and badge count. No message body, commitment text, urgent reason or file name is included; this is the `sender` preview level, and NOOK-062 adds default-on message-text previews (inside the same encrypted payload), org caps and notification actions. Push services see the endpoint, timing, encrypted packet size, TTL/normal urgency, VAPID public key and signed audience/expiry/contact URI. Subscription endpoints, public encryption keys and auth material remain private D1 records. Archives exclude keys, devices, presence and the live delivery queue; NOOK-073's body-free routing records travel in operational archives as history and are never sent, so restored installations send nothing old. Member/message/outcome erasure removes associated push records: the erased member's devices, presence, preferences, queue rows and routing records, and every recipient's queue rows and routing records of alerts the erased member, message or outcome caused. Logical erasure cannot retract packets already accepted by a push service or notices already displayed on a sleeping device.

  The installable PWA preserves Nook's UI and authenticates navigation on every notification tap. Its service worker caches no requests or private content. Permission requests require a gesture; the foreground reconciles allowed subscriptions and visibly reports re-enable failures. The previous open-tab OS notification path is removed; the title count remains. Every Web Push shows a visible notification and can update its badge. No badge-only or clear-only background Web Push is sent: WebKit revokes silent subscriptions. Foreground authoritative refresh closes read/revoked notices and updates badges through the service worker. Immediate clearing on a sleeping device, native iOS badge updates and Android native normal-priority clears remain blocked until a native transport exists. Browser acceptance is not proof of OS receipt; C8's evidence limits still apply.

LiveKit Cloud/Twilio may supply media transport/TURN; Apple (APNs, PushKit), Google (FCM) and browser vendors supply push transport, and the publisher-operated Nook Push Gateway (NOOK-078) holds the store apps' push credentials and relays sealed packets for owned installations, or the owner's own credentials and self-built apps take it out of the path; Zoom/Meet/Teams supply linked meetings. Nook owns their admission, context, failure handling and client journey. It does not operate a PSTN carrier, emergency/911 service or public webinar business; buyers obtain those services through their licensed calling/meeting provider. Huddles and native calls remain required.
- Retention, legal hold, controlled compliance export and DLP are product obligations. Legal advice, formal certification and a regulated health-data service require the organization’s counsel, qualified archive/eDiscovery partner, suitable deployment and provider agreements; a US HIPAA/BAA or certification claim needs its own evidence. Purview/export partners complement Nook’s controls and do not replace them. A hold is an explicit preservation exception to erasure, not a silently completed deletion.
- Nook owns conversation context and accepted commitments, while provider/sibling systems remain authoritative for documents, tickets, projects, CRM, calendars and incident state. Google Drive, Microsoft 365, Notion, GitHub, Jira, Asana, Salesforce, Zendesk and the linked siblings retain their source permissions and provider terms. Integration disconnection cannot delete a copy or undo a write already accepted by another service; show the remaining custody and reconciliation duty.
- Export authority is bounded by the source export and the organization’s approved audience. No adapter invents missing history or converts unknown memberships into public access. All six named incumbent/up-and-comer migrations remain obligations even where provider access, export rights or schema work is difficult.
- Offline cache and external archives require explicit owner/device custody. An offline device cannot receive a revocation before reconnect; downloaded exports, already sent notifications, screenshots and provider backups cannot be retroactively recalled. Current live erasure and protected preserved copies have distinct disclosed duties.
- Malware scanning (NOOK-061) reports signature/heuristic results: a clean label means no known threat was detected, never a guarantee. Platform limits, provider outages, US hosting/subprocessor choices and actual USD usage are disclosed. No guaranteed human response, unlimited scale, WCAG certification, end-to-end encryption or regulatory approval is implied by a local test or marketing phrase. Native apps and reliable push, media, source integrations and admin controls stay in scope until proved.
